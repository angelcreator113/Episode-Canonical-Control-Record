# §1. Owed to Sec 6 — confirm the worktree before any local read

**Ruled in session; not previously filed.**

> **After `git fetch`, assert `git rev-parse HEAD` equals
> `git rev-parse origin/main` before any local read.**
>
> The trio fetches and reads the remote ref; **it never checks the tree you are
> about to grep.** A stale worktree returns mostly-correct answers, and **can
> manufacture a finding as readily as it can hide one.**

## §1.1 The case

Sec 6's trio was run at a basis where the worktree sat **seven commits behind**
`origin/main`. `git fetch` updated the remote refs and left the checkout where
it was. Every subsequent local read — `ls`, `grep`, file existence — was against
a pre-#1099 tree while the report claimed the basis `29cee698`.

**Two errors resulted, and they were not of the same kind.**

| # | error | kind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | F-AUTH-1's tail reported as **v2.66**; actual **v2.67** | omission — a stale answer |
| 2 | A flag raised that no revision restated the FD tail — **v2.67 restates it explicitly** | **manufactured** — work reported as owed that was not owed |

**The second is the worse failure mode and the reason the rule is worth
carrying.** A stale read that hides work produces silence. **A stale read that
manufactures a finding produces a task**, and the task gets acted on before
anyone re-derives its premise.

**It surfaced by accident.** A commit title referenced a file that did not exist
on disk. Nothing in the procedure would have caught it. **Two of three revision
tails were correct, and a mostly-correct result does not announce itself.**

# §2. Owed to v25 — the blast radius of a correction

**Ruled in session; not previously filed. Register-operation class, not a PE —
it concerns how corrections behave, not the production environment.**

> **Before amending a document to match a new ruling, establish what else that
> document governs. A revision cited by more than one obligation cannot be
> amended for one of them.**

## §2.1 The two cases

**Neither was visible as damage at the moment of making it.**

| the edit that was not made | how it would have read | what it would have damaged |
|---|---|---|
| Striking `~700 disposition judgments` from v2.42, v2.43, v2.56, v2.58, v2.59 | **cleanup** — removing a figure already ruled unsound | **the numeric sort** — five in-place amendments, invisible to Sec 6's instrument, at five times the scale of the defect the carriage rule was minted to prevent |
| Amending v2.61 §4.3's per-handler language to match limb 1's ruled unit | **consistency** — aligning a procedure with a newer ruling | **FD-63's remedy** — §4.3 governs the corrected FD-63 procedure, a live separate obligation, which the edit would have silently weakened |

**Both edits would have been made in service of a correction, and both would
have looked correct while making them.** That is the whole difficulty: neither
presents as an amendment at the moment it is performed. One presents as tidying
up, the other as bringing a document into line.

## §2.2 This pairs with the carriage rule

**Both concern the blast radius of a correction, from opposite directions.**

- The **carriage rule** (`v1.49`) governs *how* a correction is carried —
  mint, do not edit in place.
- **This rule** governs *whether the target should be touched at all* — a
  document serving more than one obligation is not amendable for one of them.

**A correction that satisfies the first can still fail the second.** Minting a
new revision to carry an amendment is correct carriage, and still wrong if the
amendment reaches into a document another finding depends on.

# §3. Owed to v25 — self-describing documents and mechanical relocation

**Ruled in session; not previously filed.**

> **Self-describing documents do not survive mechanical relocation. A split,
> move, or rebase is a content change to any document that describes its own
> carriage — and the diff looks like a pure move.**

## §3.1 The case

`F-AUTH-1_Fix_Plan_v2.68.md` was authored on a branch that also carried
`a4460f25`, and its basis block said so. When the two rulings were split onto
separate branches, **that sentence became false** — the document still asserted
a branch relationship that no longer existed.

**The cherry-pick reported one file changed and nothing else.** By every
mechanical measure the move was clean. The falsehood was in prose the tooling
does not read.

**Corrected before the push**, by amending the basis block in the same commit,
so no revision of the document ever claimed the wrong branch.

## §3.2 The general form

**The class is wider than basis lines.** Any document stating its own branch,
its own PR number, its own position in a sequence, or its relationship to
another document is describing carriage — and **carriage is exactly what a
relocation changes while leaving content untouched.**

**Only noticed once.** Recorded here because the next split will look just as
clean.
